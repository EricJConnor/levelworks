# The idea list — verified, not guessed (started Sep 20 2026)

Eric's brief: he needs a product that makes money. Not necessarily trades. Genuinely new, "buy it
and use it" with little or no work from the buyer, for adults with money, buildable on what we
already have (website, Claude API, Stripe, Resend, Supabase, Meta ads; no phone/SMS/voice, no new
services, no app-store apps). Fun is welcome. Not something a person gets by typing into ChatGPT.
Quote Check stays as is until it has had its five days; this list is for what comes next.

**The rule that made this list exist:** on Sep 20 every idea pitched from memory (murder mystery
kits, group-chat wrapped, AI argument judge, school-email digest, deposit photo proof, estate item
division, letters-to-the-future, AI detective games, house manual as closing gift, pickleball
leagues) turned out to already have three to six products. **Nothing goes on this list until a
search has shown the shelf is empty**, with the competitor names written down. The list may grow to
50; each entry carries its evidence.

Two lessons from Eric, keep them: (1) never sell into the week after a death; the family is
busy and together and the busyness is doing a job; sell before or well after. (2) The only empty
shelves found so far are contracts signed in a stressful moment by someone with money, where every
"free review" on offer is secretly the other side's salesperson.

Research status: ten lanes were planned (Reddit wish-lists, Hacker News wish-lists, life-stage
pains, money pains, small-business pains, paperwork, fun/gifts, complaints about big apps,
Spanish-speaking US, new-in-2026 problems). **Two finished** (big-app complaints, new-in-2026).
The other eight were cut off by usage limits and never reported; rerun them one at a time.

## Verified: strong (shelf checked and empty, buyer has money and a deadline)

### 1. Senior-care contract check — assisted living, memory care, nursing home, CCRC, home-care agency
- **Need, in their words (AgingCare forum):** "signed the application agreement but not
  responsible party agreement because it's strongly worded indicating if I don't handle my mom's
  finances properly then I am liable." Reply: "you could be on the hook for thousands and thousands
  of dollars." Another thread: "NEVER sign as the responsible party." Another: a facility "MORE THAN
  DOUBLED her MONTHLY CARE FEE… gone to an a la carte care system which is based on points."
  https://www.agingcare.com/questions/should-i-sign-responsible-party-agreement-173003.htm
  https://www.agingcare.com/questions/can-i-refuse-to-sign-a-nursing-home-contract-as-the-responsible-party-i-signed-the-contract-under-re-483877.htm
  https://www.agingcare.com/questions/are-there-any-restrictions-on-how-much-an-assisted-living-facility-can-increase-care-costs-per-month-481070.htm
- Consumer Reports names the four traps (cost tiers, "responsible party" liability, vague
  discharge language, mandatory arbitration) and says a lawyer review costs "a few hundred dollars
  to a couple thousand." Massachusetts finalized 940 CMR 40 in July 2026 because these contracts
  were an unfair-practices problem.
- **Who:** the adult child, 50 to 65, the one signing. ~400k assisted-living move-ins a year, 4M+
  nursing-home episodes, ~2,000 CCRCs with six-figure entrance fees.
- **Why empty:** the contract arrives on move-in day in a crisis; A Place for Mom and Caring.com
  are paid by the facilities and will never flag traps; elder-law attorneys cost $300 to $3,000
  and take a week. Two searches: only law-firm pages and general guides. No AI or flat-fee product.
- **Build:** upload PDF or page photos → fixed schema (who signs and in what capacity, pricing
  method and reassessment triggers, notice for increases, discharge triggers, bed-hold, refund on
  death or move-out, arbitration, Medicaid-conversion language, entrance-fee refund schedule) →
  plain-English report, "what to write next to your signature", questions for the director, the
  state's own rules where they exist (MA, CA Title 22, NY model agreement; ~15 states cover most
  of the market). Same posture as Quote Check: software, not legal advice, a person answers.
- **Price:** $59 to $79 one-time. Meta: adults 50 to 65, assisted living / memory care /
  caregiver interests. Sell BEFORE the move-in day, to the person researching facilities.

### 2. Independent solar lease / PPA / loan check before signing
- **Need:** "My grandfather didn't even know he signed a contract because the salesman did
  everything with e-signatures… never received a paper copy" (30-year PPA on a 91-year-old).
  Texas AG April 2026: "more than 100 formal complaints and thousands more online." Sunrun class
  action: undisclosed escalator clauses, "left customers without time to read what they were
  signing." Solar Home Advocate: "average homeowner… has 3 to 5 of these red flags."
  https://shopping.yahoo.com/general/articles/distraught-grandchild-searches-advice-salesman-083000920.html
  https://lawfold.com/sunrun-solar-lawsuit/  https://solarhomeadvocate.com/contract-red-flags
- **Who:** homeowners just pitched door to door. Third-party-owned is ~45% of residential solar,
  so 250k+ new 20 to 25-year contracts a year, plus loans with hidden dealer fees.
- **Why empty:** every "free solar contract review" (DitchYourSolar, Solar Home Advocate, Solar
  Equity Solutions) is lead-gen for an exit service and only wants you after you signed;
  EnergySage sells installer leads. Nobody independent, pre-signing.
- **Build:** upload contract + photo of utility bill → rate, escalator, term, production
  guarantee, UCC-1 filing, transfer-on-sale, buyout schedule, dealer fee, the sales promises →
  full-term payment table (years 1 to 25) against a stated utility-escalation assumption, the
  rescission window (federal 3-day door-to-door rule plus state windows such as Texas SB 1036) and
  a ready-to-send cancellation letter.
- **Price:** $39. Meta: solar / Sunrun / Sunnova interests, 35 to 70, homeowners.

### 3. Insurer aerial-photo nonrenewal rebuttal dossier
- **Need:** insurers now image nearly every building and non-renew from low-res aerial photos.
  "How am I supposed to provide you with proof that I've removed mold or algae when there was no
  mold or algae that ever existed?" One insurer sent a photo of the neighbor's roof. California
  DOI counts 2.8M nonrenewal notices; Texas rates nearly doubled 2020 to 2023. California AB 75
  (right to see and dispute the image) died in committee Aug 2025, so no statutory shortcut.
  https://www.yahoo.com/news/homeowners-sound-alarm-highly-dubious-103032804.html
- **Who:** homeowners in CA, FL, TX, CO, LA with a nonrenewal or inspection letter; hundreds of
  thousands a year, about to pay more for cover.
- **Why empty:** DefensibleHome sells a $99 fill-in-the-blank template kit, "not an AI photo
  assessment tool." Roofers charge $75 to $150 for a letter and cannot write the DOI complaint.
- **Build:** upload the letter, the insurer's image if given, your dated photos, any roofer
  invoice or permit → what the insurer claims, checked against your photos → request-for-imagery
  letter, point-by-point rebuttal with exhibits, state DOI complaint draft, mitigation checklist,
  deadline reminders. Eric's contractor credibility fits this one.
- **Price:** $59. Caveat: a determined person gets 60% of this from ChatGPT; the sale is the
  packaged, state-specific, photo-checked dossier under a 30-day deadline.

## Verified: real but smaller

### 4. California deposit-recovery kit (AB 2801, in force 2025)
Landlords must now take date-stamped photos before move-in, at move-out and after repairs, and
deliver them with the itemized statement within 21 days; without them the tenant can recover the
deposit plus up to 2x for bad faith. ~17M California renters, deposits $2k to $5k, the most common
small-claims category. Searches found only law-firm explainers and the free court guide. Build:
upload the statement and photos → each deduction checked against the photo and 21-day rules and
wear-and-tear → demand letter citing Civ. Code 1950.5, exhibit list, pre-filled SC-100, reminders.
$29 to $39. Meta: "moved out in California." One late competitor search was cut off; recheck.
https://baylegal.com/ab-2801-security-deposit-photo-documentation-rules/

### 5. Medicare "what changed for me" letter reader (seasonal, Oct to Dec)
Only 36% of Medicare Advantage / Part D enrollees find the Annual Notice of Change understandable;
half never review it; 10% were forced to switch for 2026. ~35M enrollees; the buyer is often the
adult child. Brokers explain it free but only sell the plans they carry; no upload-your-letter
product found. Build: upload the ANOC, type drugs and doctors → personal diff (premium,
out-of-pocket max, drug tiers, network) and a "worth shopping this year / not" verdict. $19.
Caveat: close to what ChatGPT does with a PDF; the edge is structure and trust.

### 6. Interstate-move paperwork check
BBB: ~13,000 mover complaints a year, 57% overcharging, hostage-load scams ("final costs of
$20,437 on the original $5,000 estimate"). ~3.2M interstate moves a year. Free guides exist
(FMCSA Protect Your Move) but nothing checks YOUR estimate. Build: upload quote / order for
service → broker vs carrier, binding / non-binding / not-to-exceed, 110% rule, valuation elected,
deposit size, delivery window, arbitration, checklist. $19 to $29. Weakness: the people scammed
did not research; lowest willingness to pay on this list.

### 7. Section 128 "Trump Account contribution program" kit for small employers (new law, 2026)
Employers can contribute up to $2,500 a year per child since July 4 2026; proposed regs (Aug 11
2026) require a written plan, employee certification, a notice, a Jan 31 annual statement, W-2
Box 12 code TA. Law firms: "No model document has been issued by the IRS." ADP is "monitoring."
No self-serve document product found. Build: 10-question form → plan document, notice,
certification, trustee designation, payroll memo; each January pre-filled statements from a CSV.
$149 one-time plus $39/yr. Risks: regs are proposed (hearing Oct 15 2026); payroll vendors may
bundle it in 2027, which argues for shipping now. Owners and >2% S-corp shareholders excluded.
https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/15/trump-accounts-employer-benefits.html

### 8. IEEPA tariff-refund organiser for small importers (partial; one more competitor pass needed)
Supreme Court struck the IEEPA tariffs Feb 20 2026; CBP's CAPE refund portal opened Apr 20 2026
(~$166B owed). Refunds go only to the importer of record; other entries need a protest within 180
days. Brokers file for existing clients; no consumer-grade tool reads a pile of broker invoices,
isolates the IEEPA lines, totals the refund and tracks deadlines. $79 one-time or 1% capped at
$199. Consumers who paid courier duty on personal packages have no route: dead variant.

### 9. Trust-funding kit for DIY-trust buyers (moderate, thin user evidence)
Trusts from LegalZoom / Trust & Will go unfunded and assets end up in probate anyway; deed prep
alone is $150 to $1,500. Build: enter trust and assets → per-asset retitling letters, beneficiary
instructions, state deed guidance with a hard stop that deeds need a lawyer, nudges until done.
$39 to $59. Evidence is lawyer content, not complaining users; respect the practice-of-law line.

### 10. AB 2801 photo vault for small California landlords (borderline)
Same law as #4 from the landlord side. "A shared Google Drive folder is fully compliant," which
caps the price; TurboTenant / zInspector / RentRedi likely have condition reports (not verified).
Only as an add-on to #4.
