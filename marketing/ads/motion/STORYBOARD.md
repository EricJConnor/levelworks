# LevelWorks — "From the driveway to the bank" — storyboard

## Brief
- **For:** LevelWorks, the estimating and invoicing app for contractors, $5 a month.
- **Watching:** a US contractor, 25–60, scrolling Facebook or Instagram on a phone,
  often with the sound off. He cares about getting paid faster, looking professional to
  the client, and not doing paperwork at the kitchen table at night.
- **Do at the end:** go to levelworks.org and start the free 30 days. No card.
- **Length and sizes:** 30 seconds. 16:9 (1920×1080), 9:16 (1080×1920), 1:1 (1080×1080,
  the size the live feed-only ad set actually serves). 60 fps.
- **Brand:** LevelWorks. Spirit-level mark. Blue #2563eb as the single accent, ink
  #0b1220, white ground on #f5f7fb, green #16a34a means money in. Inter.
- **Facts:** `FACTS.md`. Nothing else goes on screen.
- **Assets:** none supplied; every screen is the app's own UI rebuilt in code from
  `app-ui.css`. No footage, no generated images.
- **References:** none supplied; motion principles from the prompt.

## The one object
A single document card. It is born as the estimate on the contractor's phone, flies to
the client and gets signed, flips into the invoice, gets paid, and its total shrinks into
the $5 chip at the end. It never leaves the frame for more than a cut.

## Signature moments (no effect names)
1. The four line items land one after another and the total in the header climbs to
   $8,200 as each one arrives, then the whole estimate lifts off the phone toward the
   viewer and is already in the client's hand when it settles.
2. On the client's phone a signature draws itself in one stroke and the status pill snaps
   from "Sent" to "Approved" in green.
3. "Paid" lands on the invoice, the balance goes to $0, and the same card slides down and
   shrinks into the "$5 a month" chip on the closing screen.

## Framing inside the phone scenes
The phone is a crop, not a device on a shelf: the pricing beat pushes in on the header and
line items, the client beat pulls back and tilts, the pay beat pushes in on the bottom of the
screen. Every move eases over ~0.7 s from the cut, and a slow push-in runs inside every shot.

## Layout per size
- 16:9: caption left third, phone right two thirds, phone height ~86% of frame.
- 9:16: caption top, phone below at ~62% of frame height; two-phone scene stacks them
  overlapping diagonally.
- 1:1: caption top, phone below, phone height ~68%.

change, one small click on each on-screen tap (Send, Sign, Pay). Music-only export too.
